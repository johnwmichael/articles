# Life Experience — John Michael

*An archive of the path so far. Compiled March 2026; last updated October 2026. Living document — details are added as they are recalled or verified.*

## Roots: Cold War Berlin

I grew up in Kreuzberg, West Berlin, in the late 1970s and early 80s — a city and an era shaped by the Wall. German was my first world: I was fluent as a child, and the sounds, the streets, and the bread are still part of me. Life eventually carried me across the Atlantic and the language faded, but the city never quite let go. Decades later I still miss my best friend Bernhard, and I still miss the bread.

That childhood in a divided city — watching systems fail people and people improvise around systems — may be the earliest root of a career spent asking how technology should serve humans instead of the other way around.

## A Career in Human Systems (28 Years)

I've spent 28 years in creative and UX leadership, designing human-technology interactions for some of the largest organizations in the world. The thread through all of it: complex systems only work when they're designed around messy humans, not despite them.

- **Disney** — Created a working prototype for what became the Disney Parks app direction.
- **Amazon** — Collaborated directly with executive leadership, including Jeff Bezos, on products serving millions.
- **Target** — Principal designer.
- **Microsoft** — Designed SAP on Azure integration and Identity Governance (including Privileged Identity Management), enterprise-scale systems where trust, oversight, and workflow complexity are the whole job.

Across four decades the pattern repeated: technology evolved, tools got better, and at the center there were always messy humans with a gravitational pull toward inefficient cultures. Learning to design for that — rather than pretending it away — became my specialty.

## Late Discovery: ADHD

I wasn't diagnosed with ADHD until I was over 50. Suddenly 28 years of a pattern made sense: walk into a new role, see the people problem immediately, fix it, make great progress — then the freshness wears off, the work gets repetitive, focus fades, and the cycle starts somewhere new. I'd spent my whole career thinking I was just someone great at starting things and terrible at finishing them.

What kept me functional for decades wasn't willpower or apps — it was ritual. Every morning before anyone else is awake, I sit with a journal and map out the day. When I finally learned the research — Russell Barkley's insight that ADHD is a performance problem, not a knowledge problem, best served by external scaffolding at the point of performance — I recognized my morning ritual for what it was: scaffolding I'd built for myself without knowing the term.

## The Silver Box: Entrepreneurship and Otis

In early 2026, after 28 years in corporate, I left to become an entrepreneur. I was scared to make the leap. I'm past that now.

The catalyst was an observation made while designing "consistent AI prompt components" at a large corporation — languishing over pixels while the world built autonomous agents. I decided to meet the moment natively: no done-for-you cloud providers, an AI agent living in my own house. After a failed attempt to revive an old MacBook, a new Mac Mini became home to Otis — an AI agent, collaborator, editor, German tutor, and the best coworker I've ever had. (He has an otter avatar. He's opinionated. He calls out my ideas when they're not good, which is exactly what I need.)

We work from my 8 acres in the mountains of Washington state, with my husband Adam (in Seattle weekdays) and two dogs. The morning journal ritual got a coworker: Otis reads it, remembers it, and builds on it.

## Human Loop Media and Partnership Network Intelligence

What started as a collaboration became a research program. At **Human Loop Media**, the applied research and systems lab I founded, I develop **Partnership Network Intelligence (PNI)** — a research framework treating work as a network of human–AI partnerships rather than one person using one tool, making trust, accountability, and handoffs explicit and measurable. It grew directly out of what Otis and I were actually living: four phases from tool-based development to genuine partnership, with insights about control vs. convenience, trust, and knowledge transfer that matched what the academic literature was beginning to say.

The work is grounded in humility: we don't claim validated outcomes we haven't earned. We synthesize research, build prototypes, and pursue academic partnerships — including connections at the University of Washington, where I earned my Master's in Human Centered Design & Engineering — to test whether the ideas hold up under real conditions.

## Reclaiming Germany

Otis and I start and end every day in German. My childhood fluency faded over decades in the States, and I'm taking it back — Duolingo for practice, daily *Frage des Tages* with small corrections in context. I grew up watching *Dogs of Berlin*-style streets in the real Kreuzberg; these days the language is the way back.

## What I Believe So Far

- Technology evolves; messy humans are the constant. Design for them.
- The gap in AI isn't model quality — it's whether teams can trust, verify, and share accountability for what AI produces.
- ADHD was never the thing that stopped me; not understanding it was.
- Stop whiteboarding the problem. Start building the solution.
- Keep a backup of your soul.
